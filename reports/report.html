PS 02: Early-Warning System for Corporate Distress and Fraud ("would it have caught IL&FS?")

Generated by python run_all.py. Every number below is read from outputs/polish_metrics.json and outputs/india_eval*.json from the same run.

1. Summary

  • Distress model (UCI Polish bankruptcy data, 43,405 firm-years): a monotone LightGBM on 28 size-free ratios that can be rebuilt from Indian filings, with train-median imputation. On the untouched holdout, PR-AUC is 0.369 against a no-skill rate of 0.069. The holdout has 410 defaults in 5,910 firm-years (6.94%, about 1:13.4). ROC-AUC is 0.823, and recall at a 5% alert rate is 0.341.
  • Indian backtest. 15 documented non-financial defaulters, 44 sector peers and a frozen universe of 893 listed companies (930 eligible borrowers at 2026-09-30). Alerts are hard-capped at min(40, 5%) per quarter.
  • Headline composite recall is 1/15 [0.00-0.32] at 4 quarters and 0/15 [0.00-0.22] at 8 quarters before the event.
  • IL&FS was not caught. IL&FS Transportation and IL&FS Engineering ranked 409/739 (cap 36), 136/739 (cap 36) at 4Q.
  • Honest bottom line. Annual book ratios, available about 6 months after year end and run through a model trained on Polish SMEs, do not separate large Indian group defaults from the many small distressed listed companies that fill a 5% alert budget. Most of the warning for these cases came from governance and market events (rating downgrades, auditor exits, results delays, pledges). These are researched for the cases (Section 5.3) and built into the escalation override. They are not counted in recall, because they were not collected for non-defaulters.

3. Method and leakage controls

  • Preprocessing fit on Polish train only: winsorise at 1/99 percentiles, then median-impute (and standardise for the logistic model). Model selection and calibration use validation only. The holdout is used once. No Indian outcome is used in any fit. Statements are used only after FY end + lag, case quarters on or after the event are dropped, and the alert cutoff is a rank, so it uses no outcomes. Automated checks: {'feature_rows_available_before_quarter': True, 'no_case_quarter_on_or_after_event': True, 'model_trained_on_polish_only': True, 'preprocessing_fit_on_polish_train_only': True, 'threshold_uses_no_outcomes': True, 'avail_lag_days': 183, 'n_quarter_rows': 37955, 'n_companies_scored': 952}.
  • Model. LightGBM with monotone sign constraints was compared with logistic regression (balanced, C=0.5). LightGBM was selected on validation PR-AUC (0.236 vs 0.148).
  • Transfer fixes, made from covariate comparison and before the frozen run. (a) In the Polish data, missingness itself predicts bankruptcy: holdout PR-AUC with native NaN is 0.561 (full64: 0.823). In Indian data, a missing value means a zero balance or an absent schedule, so the deployed model median-imputes. Zero finance cost now means uncapped interest cover. (b) Attr6 (retained earnings/TA) was dropped: its Polish median is 0.000, while Indian "reserves" include securities premium. Both fixes were found after a first run put debt-free IT companies at the top of the ranking. The fixes do not use the case labels, but they are disclosed.
  • Forensic scores. Altman Z'' = 6.56 WC/TA + 3.26 RE/TA + 6.72 EBIT/TA + 1.05 BVE/TL, with distress below 1.1. Z'' was chosen because it needs no market value (unavailable or noisy for distressed and suspended Indian stocks) and no sales/TA (sector-biased), and it is Altman's emerging-market and non-manufacturer form. On the Polish holdout, Z'' alone gives PR-AUC 0.266. Piotroski F uses 9 signals; Beneish M uses 8 variables and flags above -1.78, with missing indices set to neutral and counted.
  • Composite = model log-odds + a pre-specified log-odds overlay, which was not fitted: {'piotroski_F<=2': 0.5, 'piotroski_F=3': 0.25, 'beneish_M>-1.78': 0.3, "altman_Z''<1.1": 0.3, 'price_12m<-50%': 0.7, 'price_12m<-30%': 0.35, 'accounts_overdue_18m': 0.5}.
  • Quarterly mapping. Ratios change once a year, when the annual report becomes public. Price and overdue-accounts signals change quarterly. "4Q" means the score at the last quarter end at least 12 months before the event; "8Q" means at least 24 months.
  • Explanations. The top 3 positive contributors come from SHAP TreeExplainer log-odds plus the overlay terms, written as plain sentences with each value and its peer percentile.

Operating policy

Book. 930 real listed Indian non-financial companies scored at 2026-09-30 (frozen universe, data/reference/universe_frozen.csv; a proxy for the brief's 800-borrower book, not simulated). Alert threshold. Each quarter, flag the top k = min(40, floor(5% x N)) by composite score (this quarter: k = 40, 40 alerts). This is a hard cap, so the review team never receives more than 40 names. Quarterly cycle. 1. T+5 days: refresh filings and prices. Use only statements public by T (FY end + 183 days). Score every borrower. 2. Flagged ranks 1-10 -> ESCALATE: senior credit officer within 2 weeks; covenant test, bank-statement/GST cross-check, management call. 3. Other flagged names -> REVIEW: analyst deep-dive this quarter using the three plain-language drivers; confirm or dismiss within 30 days. 4. Ranks up to 80 -> WATCH: monthly monitoring of price, ratings and pledges. 5. Override, escalate regardless of rank: downgrade to sub-investment grade or by 2 or more notches; auditor resignation, qualification or disclaimer; results deferred past the LODR deadline; promoter pledge above 50% or up more than 10 pp in a quarter; any lender reporting SMA-1/SMA-2 (CRILC). 6. Escalation path: analyst -> credit committee (watch-list tag, limit freeze, extra security) -> CRO/Board risk committee above delegated limits -> RBI June 2019 prudential framework (inter-creditor agreement) on SMA-2 or confirmed stress.

Threshold and cost. A missed default costs 20.0 and a false alarm costs 1.0. Flagging is worth it when (1-p) x 1.0 < p x 20.0, i.e. p > 1/21 = 0.0476 (Polish validation cost optimum: 0.040). Capacity binds, so the operating rule is "top-k capped at 40; within that, everyone with PD > 1/21 is reviewed first". At an assumed 2.5% annual base rate, model-expected cost this quarter is 353.7 units with capped top-k, versus 451.3 with no reviews and 323.1 for an uncapped Bayes rule (118 alerts, which exceeds capacity).

7. Survivorship bias, sector effects, limitations

  • Survivorship bias (material). The universe was built from current listings, the Nifty Total Market and NSE lists as of October 2026. Firms that delisted, were suspended or defaulted between 2015 and 2026 are missing unless they are one of the cases. As a result, (i) historical quarters contain only survivors, so peer and universe "false alarms" are overstated as specificity (some flagged names, such as GTL, were in fact distressed), and (ii) the 5% budget is filled from survivors. A point-in-time universe from historical index constituents or exchange lists is the main fix.
  • Selection. The cases are famous large defaults, picked because they have documented dates. Small-company defaults, which the model ranks well, are under-represented. With n = 15, the confidence intervals are wide.
  • Sector effects. Infrastructure, EPC, telecom, airlines and realty structurally carry high leverage and low Z''. The cutoff is universe-wide, so a sector-relative rank is the next step.
  • Transfer gap. Polish SMEs from 2000 to 2013 are not Indian conglomerates whose subsidiaries rely on group guarantees (IL&FS, ADAG, Future).
  • Annual granularity. At the 4Q point, the latest statements are often 15-21 months old.
  • Source caveats. Screener may show restated figures. Event dates are the earliest found, and earlier defaults are possible (Videocon, McNally). The post-hoc credit-rule variant is in-sample.

8. Reproducibility

Run make setup && make all (python run_all.py), then make test (pytest). Start the local app with make app (Streamlit). python run_all.py --scrape re-fetches source pages; this changes the frozen inputs. Seed 42. No push and no deployment.
